# Revised figure legends

**Figure 1 | Similar current vaccination coverage conceals distinct cohort histories and age-structured programme-derived protection profiles.** (a) Ukraine pertussis reconstruction, 2014–2023: DTP3 coverage and reconstructed programme-derived protection gaps at ages 1–14. White diagonals trace birth cohorts; grey bands mark coverage below 30%. (b) Tanzania and Ukraine coverage histories ending at 83% DTP3 in 2023. Grey lines show countries with 2023 DTP3 coverage within ±2 percentage points of 83%; lower strips show coverage deficits. (c) Corresponding age-specific gaps. Ribbons span nine duration/partial-protection settings, not confidence intervals; open circles show cohort-weighted age-band means and rugs mark single-year ages. (d) Gap heterogeneity within disease × year × current-coverage strata containing at least two countries: residual distributions, residual dispersions and matching/weighting sensitivities. Black summaries show medians and interquartile ranges. (e) Mean age-band gaps and older-minus-younger gradients on the exact-matched country–years; cell tracks span the plotted gap scale. (f) Reconstructed gap versus preceding-decade coverage deficit, centred within matched strata. Labels give $b_{0.10}$, the change in gap proportion per 0.10 increase in deficit, with 95% country-cluster bootstrap intervals; ribbons show the same intervals. This panel reports an index decomposition and therefore has no P value. Sparse-stratum percentile intervals can exclude the original estimate.

**Figure 2 | Cohort-time gaps show disease-specific associations but do not improve temporal prediction.** (a) Adjusted rate ratios (RRs) per 0.10 larger cohort-time gap with 95% Wald confidence intervals, country counts, country–year sample sizes (n), two-sided Wald P values and Benjamini–Hochberg q values. The correction covers the four primary diseases; mumps is outside this testing family. (b) ΔAIC = AIC(alternative) − AIC(reference) between the current-coverage, coverage-history, constant-protection cohort-gap and cohort-time-gap models, fitted to identical rows within disease. Negative values favour the first-named model; rows align with the diseases in a. (c) Standardised case rates per 100,000 for all five diseases, averaged over observed covariates with country random intercepts set to zero. Curves span the 2.5th–97.5th gap percentiles; ribbons show pointwise 95% confidence intervals, grey shading the 10th–90th percentiles and rugs the observations. Gap and logarithmic rate scales differ by disease. (d) Overall, within-country and between-country RRs with 95% confidence intervals (top), and peak-normalised distributions of leave-one-country-out log(RRs) (bottom), for all five diseases. Columns in c and d run left to right as measles, rubella, pertussis, diphtheria and mumps, matching a. (e) ΔAIC = AIC(cohort-time-gap model) − AIC(coverage-history model) across protection-duration and partial-series-protection settings; negative values favour the cohort-time-gap model and asterisks mark the primary settings. (f) ΔAIC = AIC(cohort-time-gap model) − AIC(coverage-history model) versus disease-stratified RMSE change from pooled temporal holdouts: 100 × [RMSE(cohort-time gap)/RMSE(coverage history) − 1]. Positive RMSE changes indicate worse prediction; shading marks better contemporaneous fit but worse prediction. Disease colours are consistent across panels. AIC, Akaike information criterion; RMSE, root mean squared error.

**Figure 3 | Reconstructed cohort gaps and the country and age distribution of EU/EEA measles cases.** (a) Monthly reported measles incidence per 100,000 in the EU/EEA from 2016 to June 2026 using the WHO/UNICEF epidemic-curve archive: EU/EEA total (grey), the ten leading countries (purple) and the remaining countries (teal), each aggregated over the countries reporting in that month. (b) The ten EU/EEA countries with the most reported cases from January 2024 to June 2026 (log scale) and (c) their 2025 WUENIC MCV1 and MCV2 coverage; the dashed line marks 95%. (d) Within-country standardised monthly incidence from 2016 to June 2026 for those countries; the Z-score is calculated separately for each country and grey cells indicate missing country-month records. (e) Reconstructed 2023 cohort gap (%) by age band for the ten countries, with the EU/EEA median; grey cells are bands below 90% reconstruction completeness. (f) Correlations of mean annual 2024–2026 incidence with 2023 coverage across 29 EU/EEA countries (shaded, unadjusted) and rank partial correlations with 2023 age-band gaps conditional on 2023 WUENIC MCV1 and MCV2. Bars are 95% country-bootstrap percentile intervals from 2,000 country resamples. (g) Primary within-country age alignment of ECDC case-based data from 2023–2025: closed 1–29-year bands, with the gap for age $a-1$ in the preceding year tracking the same birth cohort. Log2 observed/expected cases from a null negative-binomial model with country-year and age-band fixed effects are plotted against the gap after removing those effects; size is expected cases. Lines and shaded bands show gap effects (IRR per 10 percentage points) and model-based Wald 95% CIs for all EU/EEA (21 countries, 40 country-years, 33,273 cases; grey) and the ten leading countries (10 countries, 26 country-years, 32,872 cases; purple); teal points are the remaining countries (11 countries, 14 country-years, 401 cases), whose separate estimate is given in Supplementary Table S21. The added-variable view of the 1–4-year gap and the screening-equivalent coverage plot are provided in Supplementary Fig. S12. All analyses are ecological and quantify cohort–case alignment rather than outbreak causation.

**Figure 4 | Paired history and waning scenarios for expected reports.** (a) Posterior mean annual expected reports, equally averaged across ten countries, for the observed history and the 10–14-year high-protection reference (“10–14 y reference”), each with and without waning. The reference caps gaps at 0.10 at ages 10–14 only and represents a controlled model history. Disease facets have separate logarithmic scales; country trajectories are in Supplementary Figs. S4–S8. (b) Paired waning-minus-no-waning differences in cumulative 2014–2023 expected reports, calculated as the equally weighted mean across the ten countries. Half-densities show draw distributions on a signed logarithmic scale with a linear segment between −1,000 and +1,000 reports; points and bars show medians and central 95% posterior intervals. Measles and rubella are zero by construction. (c) Pooled reductions relative to observed history under waning for the 10–14-year high-protection reference (squares) and five-year recovery (triangles); country totals are summed within draws before calculating ratios. (d) Country-specific percentage increases under waning relative to no waning. (e) Cumulative scenario totals relative to observed history (100%); dashed line and shading mark recovery onset and transition. Points, lines and intervals in c–e show medians and central 95% posterior intervals. (f) Observed reports versus held-out negative-binomial predictive medians, and (g) coverage of 95% observation-predictive intervals. Calibration uses 2014–2019; testing uses 2020–2023. Expected-report intervals exclude observation noise and fixed-input uncertainty. Scenario contrasts are conditional on the fitted model.

**Figure 5 | Under the specified durations, waning shifts protection gaps towards older children and alters contact-weighted transmission potential.** (a) Country-mean protection gaps at ages 0–4 versus 10–14, averaged over 2014–2023. Open/filled points denote no waning/waning; thin lines connect paired country states, while larger points show equal-country means. The dashed identity line and shaded region mark equal gaps and larger older-child gaps, respectively. Concentric points indicate identical scenarios. (b) Waning-minus-no-waning change in mean single-age acquisition susceptibility at ages 1–14 by calendar year, in percentage points. (c) The same gap change routed through recipient acquisition susceptibility and source onward infectiousness by age band. (d) Waning-minus-no-waning change in the contact-weighted operator diag(s) C diag(ω), with recipient ages in rows and source ages in columns; weights share a normalisation across diseases and scenarios and use a square-root colour scale. Marginal sums are in Supplementary Fig. S9. (e) Change in normalised dominant-mode age contributions; negative changes indicate smaller relative contributions. Horizontal lines are 95% paired country-bootstrap intervals. (f) Country-mean absolute changes in initial Rₑ over 2014–2023, using the same country order ranked by pertussis; horizontal lines are 95% within-country year-bootstrap intervals. These are deterministic structural comparisons; intervals reflect resampling under fixed parameters. Rₑ, effective reproduction number.

**Figure 6 | Programme-derived and serological profiles provide complementary age-pattern evidence.** (a) Changes in survey-weighted NHANES 2017–March 2020 seronegativity and 2018 programme-derived gaps relative to the youngest included age: 8 years for measles/mumps and 6 for rubella. Projected model ages are excluded. Coloured solid and grey dashed lines show seronegativity and programme gaps; labels give descriptive endpoint changes. (b) Source-specific older-minus-younger MMR seropositivity contrasts. ESEN2 small points show country profiles, open circles medians and segments interquartile ranges; counts indicate positive contrasts. The mumps display includes the Swedish profile with nonstandard source ages; exclusion gives 15/15 positive contrasts (Supplementary Tables S43 and S44). Squares and triangles show KiGGS and PIENTER2 estimates. Age comparisons differ by source and are not pooled (Supplementary Table S43). (c) Kilifi, Kenya, 2021 age-band comparisons: external markers versus programme gaps. Diphtheria shows minimum-threshold antitoxin protection with published confidence intervals; pertussis shows anti-PT ≥62.5 EU/mL as a recent-exposure marker, without a derived combined interval. Antibody markers and programme gaps are distinct estimands; panel c compares their age profiles without treating one as a measurement of the other. NHANES, National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey; MMR, measles–mumps–rubella; anti-PT, anti-pertussis toxin.

**Supplementary Figure S2 | Reconstruction and observation robustness.** (A) Fraction of all country–years satisfying the complete fourteen-age or at-least-80% reconstruction criteria, by year, using strict component evidence; mumps here uses the strict component layer described in the vaccine mapping in Methods. (B) Composition of case-status rows over 2014–2023; explicit reported zeros and unreported fields remain separate. (C) Distribution of within-stratum gap standard deviations for exact disease × year × current-coverage strata with at least two countries; each histogram counts strata, with disease-specific vertical scales. Sparse recovery contrasts are omitted from the publication figure and retained only in the analysis archive. (F) Joined, missing-input and complete model-row counts. Recovery contrasts are descriptive summaries of the reconstruction.
